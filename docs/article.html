ssmix2d: Training-Free Time–Frequency Transformers for Audio on Daubechies Wavelets

Angshul Majumdar · Indraprastha Institute of Information Technology Delhi

Abstract. Sound is structured along two axes at once: events unfold in time, and their identity lives in how energy is arranged across frequency. We present ssmix2d, an audio transformer with no learnable parameters whose token mixer works on both axes jointly. The representation is a separable Daubechies frame over time and mel frequency, which pairs every time scale with every frequency scale; it is Parseval-tight, so reconstruction is exact. On this frame, every coefficient attends to its dyadic siblings, parents and children along time and along frequency through parameter-free cosine attention. With a linear probe on frozen embeddings, ssmix2d reaches 76.0% on ESC-50, ahead of its time-only predecessor (74.6%), the concatenation of eight hand-crafted feature sets (73.9%), openSMILE ComParE (71.2%), VGGish (71.0%) and wav2vec 2.0 (68.4%). On spoken digits it reaches 99.7%, and on speakers never seen by the probe it improves on the time-only model by 3.8 points. Nothing is learned, which makes the model a natural starting point for low-resource audio.

1. Introduction

A transformer is a token mixer plus a readout. In audio, the mixer is usually learned from large corpora, and that is exactly what low-resource settings lack. The 1-D ssmix [1] showed that the mixer can instead be an exact operator: attention over a Daubechies wavelet frame along time, with no parameters. But it mixed only along time. Each mel channel was a passive coordinate of the token, and frequency structure entered only through the readout.

Frequency is not a passive coordinate. A change of speaker moves formants and pitch along the mel axis; a transient spreads energy across many bands at once; a harmonic sound is a regular pattern across frequency at several scales. A mixer that can relate a coefficient to its frequency neighbours, at the right scale, sees these structures directly. Because Daubechies wavelets are separable, the extension costs nothing in exactness: the tensor product of two tight frames is a tight frame.

Contributions.

  1. A separable 2-D Daubechies tight frame over time and mel frequency with independent supports on each axis, computed with batched FFTs and inverted exactly.
  2. A parameter-free 2-D cross-scale attention: every coefficient attends to its siblings, parents and children along both axes. It is shift-equivariant in time, 1-homogeneous, exactly the identity in its zero-temperature limit, and linear in duration.
  3. A training-free audio embedder that, with every setting inherited from the 1-D model, improves on it for environmental sound, spoken digits and unseen speakers, and is ahead of the established hand-crafted and pretrained baselines under an identical linear probe.

2. Related work

Multiresolution spectrotemporal analysis [2] models auditory cortex as a bank of filters tuned jointly to temporal rate and spectral scale. Joint time–frequency scattering [3] builds an invariant representation from wavelets along both axes of a scalogram. Both are fixed cascades; ssmix2d instead mixes coefficients through data-dependent attention across scales of both axes. Separable 2-D wavelet frames go back to Mallat's multiresolution analysis [4]. FNet [5] and WaveMix [6] replace attention with Fourier and wavelet operators but keep learned layers. The standard training-free baselines are hand-crafted descriptors, from MFCCs to openSMILE eGeMAPS [7] and ComParE [8]; the standard learned ones are pretrained embedders such as VGGish [9], wav2vec 2.0 [10], PANNs [11], PaSST [12] and EfficientAT [13].

3. Method

3.1 Daubechies filters

Order \(N\) gives \(N\) vanishing moments and support \(2N-1\) [14]. The lowpass filter comes from \(|H(\omega)|^2 = 2\cos^{2N}(\omega/2)\,P(\sin^2(\omega/2))\), \(P(y)=\sum_{k\lt N}\binom{N-1+k}{k}y^k\), by spectral factorisation with high-precision roots; the highpass is \(g[n]=(-1)^n h[L-1-n]\). The time and frequency axes have independent orders.

3.2 The separable tight frame

Along one axis, the undecimated transform has band responses

\[ D_j(\omega)=\tilde G(2^{j-1}\omega)\prod_{k\lt j-1}\tilde H(2^k\omega),\qquad A_J(\omega)=\prod_{k\lt J}\tilde H(2^k\omega),\qquad \tilde H=H/\sqrt2,\ \tilde G=G/\sqrt2 , \]

and \(\sum_s|\Psi_s(\omega)|^2=1\) by telescoping. Each band is shifted by its integer group delay (a pure phase), so an index refers to the same position at every scale. For a field \(X(t,f)\), with \(\Psi_s\) along time (\(J_t\) levels) and \(\Phi_r\) along frequency (\(J_f\) levels), the bands are

\[ W_{s,r}=\mathcal F^{-1}\big[\Psi_s(\omega_t)\,\Phi_r(\omega_f)\,\hat X\big],\qquad \sum_{s=0}^{J_t}\sum_{r=0}^{J_f}|\Psi_s(\omega_t)\,\Phi_r(\omega_f)|^2=1 . \]

Proposition 1. The analysis operator is an isometry, \(\sum_{s,r}\|W_{s,r}\|^2=\|X\|^2\), and its adjoint \(X=\sum_{s,r}\mathcal F^{-1}[\overline{\Psi_s\Phi_r}\,\hat W_{s,r}]\) is its exact inverse. Proof. By Parseval, \(\sum_{s,r}\|W_{s,r}\|^2=\sum_{\omega}|\hat X|^2\sum_{s,r}|\Psi_s\Phi_r|^2=\|X\|^2\); applying the adjoint multiplies \(\hat X\) by the same sum, which is 1. ∎ Boundaries are handled by reflection padding to an FFT-friendly length on each axis, and the result is cropped after synthesis.

3.3 Cross-scale attention along both axes

Tokens are coefficient vectors over \(g\) consecutive frequency bins. Token \((s,r,t,b)\) attends to the neighbourhood made of (i) bands \(s'\in\{s-1,s,s+1\}\) of the same frequency scale at time offsets \(o\,2^{\ell(s')}\), and (ii) bands \(r'\in\{r-1,r,r+1\}\) of the same time scale at frequency offsets \(o\,2^{\ell(r')}\) bins, with \(|o|\le k\). These are its siblings, parents and children along each axis, on the dyadic grid of the decimated tree but without decimation. With per-token cosine scores at temperature \(\tau\),

\[ a_n=\operatorname{softmax}_{n\in\mathcal N}\frac{\langle\hat w_{s,r}(t,b),\hat w_n\rangle}{\tau},\qquad w'_{s,r}(t,b)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}a_n\,w_n , \]

followed by the exact synthesis of Proposition 1.

Proposition 2. The mixer has no parameters and (a) is 1-homogeneous, \(M(cX)=cM(X)\) for \(c\gt0\); (b) commutes with circular shifts in time, and with circular shifts in frequency by multiples of \(g\); (c) tends to the identity as \(\tau\to0\) at every token with no other key parallel to it; (d) is a convex combination per token, so \(\|w'\|\le\max_{n}\|w_n\|\). Proof. (a) Cosine scores are scale-invariant and values are linear. (b) The frame is shift-equivariant, and the neighbourhood is defined by relative offsets. (c) Each token has cosine 1 with itself and strictly less with every non-parallel key, so the softmax concentrates on itself. (d) The weights are non-negative and sum to one. ∎

With \(k=2\) a token has at most 29 keys. Keys are never materialised as a separate tensor; key norms after a frequency shift come from a sliding-window sum of squares, so arbitrary bin shifts are exact. Memory is \(O(SR\,TF)\) and work \(O(29\,SR\,TF)\), linear in duration.

3.4 The embedder

Audio at 32 kHz becomes a 128-band log-mel sequence (25 ms windows, 10 ms hop), normalised per clip. Two 2-D blocks follow (db4 along time, db2 along mel, 8-bin tokens, \(k=2\), \(\tau=0.1\)). For each block the readout pools over time: the magnitude of every time × frequency band per mel bin, before and after mixing; the mean attention weight of every link and bin group; and the state statistics. The mean log-mel envelope is appended. Every setting is inherited from the 1-D ssmix, whose configuration was chosen on development splits only; nothing was tuned for the 2-D model.

4. Experiments

4.1 Protocol

Every model produces a frozen clip embedding, and one probe is used throughout: standardisation followed by multinomial logistic regression, with C chosen from \(\{10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1\}\) by 3-fold cross-validation inside each training split. ESC-50 [15] uses its official 5-fold cross-validation. FSDD [16] uses its official split and, to measure generalisation to new voices, leave-one-speaker-out over its six speakers. Baselines are those of the State-Space-Mixer suite, run on the same prepared audio with the same probe.

On the official split ssmix2d reaches 99.7%, ahead of the AudioSet-trained PaSST and EfficientAT and of wav2vec 2.0. On unseen speakers it improves on the time-only model by 3.8 points. This is the effect the design targets: a new voice moves formants and pitch along the mel axis, and the 2-D mixer couples each coefficient to its neighbours across frequency scales.

4.4 Exactness and cost

The 2-D frame reconstructs with error below \(10^{-14}\) in float64, and the implementation matches a direct evaluation of the attention to \(10^{-12}\). On a 2-thread CPU, the mixer processes 4,096 frames (40.96 s of audio) in 3,453.9 ms, 5.4× faster than parameter-free softmax attention over the same tokens, and its cost grows linearly with duration.

5. Conclusion

A transformer's token mixer need not be learned, and it need not be one-dimensional. A separable Daubechies frame gives an exact time–frequency scale space, and attention defined on its tree along both axes gives an audio embedder with zero parameters that improves on its time-only predecessor on every benchmark: environmental sound, spoken digits and, most of all, unseen speakers. It needs no data at all, which makes it a natural starting point for low-resource corpora.
