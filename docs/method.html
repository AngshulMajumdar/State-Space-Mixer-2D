Method

Three exact building blocks: Daubechies filters of controllable support on each axis, a separable tight frame over time and frequency, and a parameter-free cross-scale attention that moves along both axes.

1. Daubechies filters of any order

Order \(N\) gives \(N\) vanishing moments and support \(2N-1\). The lowpass filter is obtained from \(|H(\omega)|^2 = 2\cos^{2N}(\omega/2)\,P(\sin^2(\omega/2))\) with \(P(y)=\sum_{k\lt N}\binom{N-1+k}{k}y^k\), by spectral factorisation at high precision. The highpass is \(g[n] = (-1)^n h[L-1-n]\). The time axis and the frequency axis each get their own order.

2. A separable tight frame over time and frequency

Along one axis, the undecimated, delay-aligned Daubechies frame has band responses \(\Psi_s(\omega)\), \(s=0,\dots,J\), with \(\sum_s|\Psi_s(\omega)|^2=1\) (the sum telescopes because \(|\tilde H|^2+|\tilde G|^2=1\)). For a field \(X(t,f)\) the 2-D bands are the products

\[ \Psi_{s,r}(\omega_t,\omega_f)=\Psi_s(\omega_t)\,\Phi_r(\omega_f),\qquad \sum_{s,r}|\Psi_{s,r}|^2=\Big(\sum_s|\Psi_s|^2\Big)\Big(\sum_r|\Phi_r|^2\Big)=1 , \]

so the 2-D frame is Parseval-tight and its adjoint is the exact inverse. Every time scale is paired with every frequency scale, so a fine time scale can meet a coarse frequency scale (a transient with a broad spectrum) and vice versa (a slowly varying, narrow-band partial). Both axes are computed with batched FFTs on the DFT grid, at a cost that does not depend on the filter supports.

3. Cross-scale attention along both axes

Tokens are coefficient vectors over \(g\) consecutive frequency bins: \(w_{s,r}(t,b)\in\mathbb R^g\) for band \((s,r)\), instant \(t\) and bin group \(b\). Token \((s,r,t,b)\) attends to

\[ \mathcal N=\{(s',r,\,t+o\,2^{\ell(s')},\,b) : s'\in\{s-1,s,s+1\}\}\ \cup\ \{(s,r',\,t,\,b\text{ shifted by }o\,2^{\ell(r')}\text{ bins}) : r'\in\{r-1,r,r+1\}\},\quad |o|\le k , \]

its siblings, parents and children along time and along frequency (\(\ell\) is the dyadic level of a band). With per-token cosine weights

\[ a_n=\operatorname{softmax}_{n\in\mathcal N}\frac{\langle\hat w_{s,r}(t,b),\hat w_n\rangle}{\tau},\qquad w'_{s,r}(t,b)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal N}a_n\,w_n , \]

followed by the exact 2-D synthesis. Every key is normalised over its own bins after shifting, so frequency shifts that are not multiples of \(g\) are exact. The operator has no parameters. It is shift-equivariant in time (and in frequency for shifts by multiples of \(g\)) and 1-homogeneous, and it tends to the identity as \(\tau\to0\). With \(k=2\) a token has at most \(1+14+14=29\) keys. Memory is \(O(SR\,TF)\) and work \(O(29\,SR\,TF)\): linear in duration.

4. Audio embedder

AudioEmbedder2D runs at 32 kHz with 128 mel bands (25 ms window, 10 ms hop) and per-clip normalisation. Two 2-D cross-scale mixer blocks follow: db4 along time, db2 along mel, 8-bin tokens, \(k=2\), \(\tau=0.1\). For each block, the readout pools over time:

  • the magnitude of every time × frequency band, per mel bin, before and after mixing;
  • the mean attention weight of every link and bin group: how strongly each band couples to its neighbours in time scale and in frequency scale;
  • the state statistics.

The mean log-mel envelope is appended once, at the end. All settings are inherited unchanged from the 1-D ssmix, whose configuration was chosen on development splits only.
